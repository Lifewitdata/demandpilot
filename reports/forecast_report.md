# DemandPilot -- forecast and inventory evaluation

Generated by `python -m src.evaluate`. Data: UCI Online Retail II (2009-11-30 to
2011-12-05, 106 weeks), top 100 SKUs by volume
(20% of revenue). Model: HistGradientBoosting,
direct multi-step, 8-week horizon, 20 leakage-safe features.
Artifact: `demand_hgb_v1.joblib` (version v1).

## Holdout (last 8 weeks, never used for fitting or calibration)

| model | wape | mase_macro | bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| hgb_bias_corrected | 0.566 | 0.865 | -0.033 |
| hgb | 0.553 | 0.848 | -0.095 |
| ma4 | 0.591 | 0.911 | -0.135 |
| naive | 0.686 | 1.050 | -0.098 |
| snaive | 0.972 | 1.339 | 0.484 |

WAPE = volume-weighted absolute error; MASE uses each SKU's seasonal-naive training MAE.
The naive policy is beaten by 84% of SKUs; the whole holdout falls inside the
October-December holiday ramp, which all models under-forecast (negative bias) --
the fold-calibrated bias correction halves the model's bias at a 0.004 WAPE cost.

## Inventory policy (lead time 4w +
review 2w = 6-week protection window)

Monte Carlo (20,000 draws/SKU) over validation-fold
residuals; holding cost 0.02/unit-week vs
stockout cost 2.0/unit. The naive policy
achieves only a 76% pooled service level:

| policy | safety stock (units) | cost per cycle | vs naive |
|---|---|---|---|
| naive 2-weeks-of-cover | 34577 | 18290 | - |
| forecast-driven @ target SL 0.95 | 81254 | 11989 | 34.4% cheaper |
| forecast-driven @ per-SKU optimum (min cost at SL 0.93) | 72996 | 11982 | 34.5% cheaper |

The empirical cost minimum around SL 0.93 matches the newsvendor critical
ratio implied by the cost parameters (2.0 /
(2.0 + 0.02 x 6) = 0.94),
a useful sanity check on the simulation.

![service level curve](figures/service_level_curve.png)

## Limitations

- Weekly SKU demand is intermittent; WAPE of ~0.55 is high in absolute terms and is why
  reorder decisions, not point forecasts, are the product.
- Holdout bias is negative (holiday ramp); production would recalibrate bias factors
  on a rolling window.
- The Monte Carlo treats horizon residuals as independent across weeks; demand
  autocorrelation would fatten tails further, so computed safety stock is a
  lower bound under that correlation.
- Non-product codes (M, DOT, POST, ...) carry ~3.4% of revenue; the volume-based
  top-100 selection excludes all of them naturally, but a production catalogue
  would need explicit mapping.
